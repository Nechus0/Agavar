How to play

What is it about?

You design a bacterium and put it on an agar plate with opponents. After that everything runs on its own – you can only watch (and fast-forward). Whoever is left at the end wins.

Career

Each of your bacteria has its own career. It starts with only 6 upgrade points and the simplest movements. Every level has a special opponent – a real germ with a typical ability. Beat it and your bacterium gets 2 new upgrade points and may use that ability from now on (e.g. spores from Bacillus subtilis or toxins from Staphylococcus aureus). You can redistribute your points at any time.

Stars, atlas and achievements

Every level has three stars: for winning, for a fast win (under 12 h) and for covering 25 % of the plate at some point. All three give a bonus point. Every germ you beat goes into your atlas with a picture and a fact, and special feats earn achievements.

Free play

Here you set the point budget yourself and can spend all points right away. Special abilities and special movements are only available once you have unlocked them in a career.

What happens on the plate?

Every coloured pixel is a bacterial cell. Cells eat the medium below them (bright yellow = lots of food, dark = eaten up), move according to their pattern, divide when they have enough energy, and die of hunger or old age. When a cell runs into an enemy, they fight: attack against defense plus a little luck.

Spotting special abilities

Capsule: cells look brighter while their slime layer is intact – they survive one deadly attack. Photosynthesis: cells shimmer and gain energy from light. Predator: red bite marks show where enemies were eaten. Spores are pale, almost white dots. Biofilm is a dark, patterned mat. Toxins show as a toxic green cloud around the colony.

Chemotaxis is not a special ability but a trait (level 0–4): the higher it is, the more often cells head straight for food.

Movement

Every cell follows the same pattern. When a cell divides, its daughter starts right next to it and continues the pattern – that is how a colony grows. The editor shows one cell and its offspring on the left and the whole colony on a plate on the right. More movements and bigger patterns are unlocked in your career.

End of the game

A round lasts at most 48 hours of incubation (the clock runs at the top). You win if you are the last culture alive, make up almost all cells for a long time – or have the most cells when time runs out.

Install on your iPhone

Open in Safari, tap Share and choose “Add to Home Screen”. Agava then starts like a full-screen app and also works offline. Your bacteria are only saved on this device.
